# How Your Commission Is Calculated — A Simple Guide

This guide explains, in the simplest way, how you (a selling agent) earn money
when you bring an investor to Ekush. Keep it handy for when an investor or a new
agent asks "how do the fees work?"

## The rates (read this first)

Ekush has **three funds**, and they fall into **two groups**. The group decides
the **trail** rate:

| Fund | Full name | Group |
|---|---|---|
| **EFUF** | Ekush First Unit Fund | Equity |
| **EGF** | Ekush Growth Fund | Equity |
| **ESRF** | Ekush Stable Return Fund | Fixed income |

**Standard commission rates:**

| | **EFUF & EGF** (equity) | **ESRF** (fixed income) |
|---|---|---|
| **Upfront** (one time, on new money) | **0.20%** | **0.20%** |
| **Trail — Year 1** (per year) | **0.40%** | **0.20%** |
| **Trail — Year 2 onwards** (per year) | **0.35%** | **0.15%** |

Reading a rate: **0.20% means 2 taka for every 1,000 taka.** 0.40% means 4 taka for
every 1,000 taka, and so on.

> ⚠️ **Your rates may be different.** The table above is the *standard* rate. Your own
> rates are written in **your Selling Agent Agreement** and shown on your dashboard —
> some agents have different numbers. If your dashboard shows something else, **your
> agreement is the real one.** The examples below use these standard rates just to
> make the maths easy to follow.

---

## Two kinds of fee — the whole idea in one picture

You earn **two** different fees. They are not the same thing.

| | **Upfront** | **Trail** |
|---|---|---|
| When? | **One time**, when NEW money comes in | **Every month**, again and again |
| Paid on? | The **new** money the investor adds | How much they are **holding right now** |
| Think of it as | A **thank-you for bringing money in** | A **thank-you for keeping it with us** |

### The water line (please remember this one picture)

Imagine your investor's money is **water in a glass**. On the wall behind the
glass we draw a **line** at the **highest the water has ever reached**.

- 🖊️ **The line only ever goes UP. It never comes down.**
- 💰 **You earn upfront ONLY when the water rises ABOVE the line** (a brand-new record).
- The fancy name for this line is a **"high-water mark"**. That's all it means:
  the highest point ever reached.

**One more big rule (the new one):** we look at **all three funds together as one
glass**. EFUF, EGF and ESRF are added up into a single total. This stops the same
money from earning upfront twice — you'll see why in Example 3.

Let's follow one investor, **Mr. Rahim**, whom **you** brought to Ekush.

---

## Starting point — Rahim invests for the first time

Rahim puts **1,00,000 taka** into Fund EFUF.

- His total money with you: **1,00,000** (his highest ever)
- The **line goes up to 1,00,000**
- New money above the old line = **1,00,000**
- **Upfront = 1,00,000 × 0.20% = 200 taka.** 🎉 You earn **200**.

Now the line sits at **1,00,000**. Remember that number for every example below.

---

## Example 1 — Rahim withdraws the FULL amount

Rahim takes out **all 1,00,000**. His glass is now **empty (0)**.

- The **line stays at 1,00,000** (it never comes down).
- You already earned your **200** upfront. ✅ **You keep it.** Nobody takes it back.
- **No new upfront** — nothing went *above* the line.
- **Trail stops.** Trail is paid on money he is holding. He is holding nothing now,
  so from the day he withdraws, there is **no more monthly trail** on that money.

**In one line:** taking money out does **not** cost you the upfront you already
earned, but it **does** stop future trail.

---

## Example 2 — Rahim withdraws PART of it

Rahim takes out **40,000**, leaving **60,000** in the glass.

- The **line stays at 1,00,000**.
- **No new upfront** (60,000 is below the line).
- 👉 **Important:** if Rahim later puts money back, he must climb **above 1,00,000**
  before you earn upfront again. Going from 60,000 back up to 1,00,000 is just
  **refilling below the line** → **0 upfront**.
- **Trail shrinks.** Trail is now calculated on **60,000** worth of units instead of
  1,00,000. Less money held = smaller monthly trail.

**In one line:** a partial withdrawal lowers the water but **not the line**, so the
refill earns you nothing until it beats the old record.

---

## Example 3 — Rahim SWITCHES funds (this is the key one)

Rahim moves his **60,000 out of EFUF** and puts the same **60,000 into EGF**, on
the same day. He hasn't added any new money — he just **moved it to a different
fund**.

Because we count **all funds together as one glass**:

| Step | What happens | Combined total |
|---|---|---|
| Start | 60,000 sitting in EFUF | 60,000 |
| Sell EFUF | takes 60,000 out | 0 |
| Buy EGF | puts 60,000 in | 60,000 |

- His combined total is **still 60,000** — the **same money**, just moved house.
- The **line is still at 1,00,000**. 60,000 is below it.
- **New money above the line = 0 → Upfront = 0.** ✅

**Why this matters:** the same 60,000 does **not** pay you upfront a second time
just because it changed funds. (In the old system each fund had its *own* line, so
EGF's line started at 0 and the 60,000 looked "brand new" — you would have been paid
twice on money that never actually left. That is now fixed.)

**In one line:** moving money between funds is **not new money**, so it earns **no**
upfront.

---

## Example 4 — Rahim adds MORE, into a different fund (combined water line)

Rahim now holds **60,000 in EGF**. The line is at **1,00,000**.

He adds **70,000 of brand-new money** into a third fund, **ESRF**.

- Combined total = 60,000 + 70,000 = **1,30,000**.
- That is **above** the old line of 1,00,000. 🎉 A new record!
- **New money above the line = 1,30,000 − 1,00,000 = 30,000.**
- **Upfront = 30,000 × 0.20% = 60 taka.** You earn **60**.
- The **line moves up to 1,30,000**.

👀 **Look carefully:** Rahim added **70,000**, but you earned upfront on only
**30,000**. Why? Because **40,000 of it just refilled the space below the old line**
(remember, he was 40,000 short of his record after the earlier withdrawal). Only the
part that pushed him to a **brand-new high** counts.

**Which fund's rate?** We use the rate of the fund the **new money went into** —
here, ESRF. (If new money lands in two funds at once, we split it and use each
fund's own rate.)

**In one line:** adding money counts as new **only for the part that beats the old
record**, and it's measured across all funds together.

---

## Example 5 — Adding money and how TRAIL is calculated

Trail is the **other** fee — the small **monthly** one. It has nothing to do with
the water line. It is simply: **"a tiny slice of what Rahim is holding, every
month, for as long as he stays."**

Right now Rahim holds money in **two** funds, and remember **each group has its own
trail rate**:

- **EGF (equity):** about **60,000** — Year-1 trail rate **0.40%** per year
- **ESRF (fixed income):** about **70,000** — Year-1 trail rate **0.20%** per year

**How we work out one month's trail:**

A year has 12 months, so we take the yearly rate and divide by 12 to get one month's
slice. We do each fund with **its own** rate, then add them up.

| Fund | Held | Monthly rate (yearly ÷ 12) | This month's trail |
|---|---|---|---|
| EGF (equity) | 60,000 | 0.40% ÷ 12 = 0.0333% | 60,000 × 0.0333% ≈ **20 taka** |
| ESRF (fixed) | 70,000 | 0.20% ÷ 12 = 0.0167% | 70,000 × 0.0167% ≈ **12 taka** |
| **Total** | | | **≈ 32 taka this month** |

You earn roughly that **every single month**, just for Rahim staying invested.

- If Rahim **adds more**, his holding grows → **next month's trail grows** too.
- If Rahim **withdraws**, his holding shrinks → **trail shrinks**.
- After **one year** with a fund, that fund's rate steps down to the Year-2 rate
  (0.35% for equity, 0.15% for fixed income), so the monthly slice becomes a little
  smaller.
- We actually use the **average** value across the period (not one single day), so a
  fair number is used even as the NAV moves up and down.

**In one line:** trail is a small monthly thank-you based on **how much he holds in
each fund**, each fund at its own rate; it keeps coming as long as he stays, and it
goes up or down as his balance does.

---

## The five golden rules (the whole guide on one hand ✋)

1. **The line only goes up.** You earn upfront only when the investor reaches a
   **brand-new high**, counting all three funds together.
2. **Taking money out never takes back upfront you already earned** — but it lowers
   future trail.
3. **Refilling below the old line earns nothing** until it beats the record.
4. **Switching funds is not new money** → no upfront. **Only genuinely new money**
   that sets a new high earns upfront.
5. **Trail is the monthly thank-you** on whatever they're holding — bigger balance,
   bigger trail; smaller balance, smaller trail; stops when they fully withdraw.

---

## A couple of extra notes (for the curious)

- **Reinvested dividends (CIP) are not counted as new money.** If Rahim's own
  dividend is put back into the fund, that is his money coming back — not money you
  brought — so it does **not** raise the line and does **not** earn upfront.
- **Direct subscriptions earn no agent commission.** If an investor puts money in
  directly (not through you), that money is not yours to earn on.
- **These are estimates until the office posts them.** What you see on your
  dashboard is a live estimate. The actual amount is confirmed when the office runs
  the monthly commission.

*Your exact rates and terms are in your Selling Agent Agreement. If a number here
ever seems different from your dashboard, ask the office — the office record is
always the final word.*
